Four
directions outward.

Four appears whenever systems organize into squares, quartets, four-state alphabets, or four coordinates—but each appearance has its own reason. This room connects them without pretending they share a mystical cause.

A C G T

DNA alphabet

DNA sequences are written with four nucleotide bases: adenine, cytosine, guanine, and thymine. A purely combinatorial string of n DNA-base positions has 4ⁿ possible letter sequences before biological constraints are considered.

◇

Tetrahedron

The regular tetrahedron has four vertices and four triangular faces. It is the 3-dimensional simplex—the simplest polytope that encloses volume in ordinary three-dimensional Euclidean space.

4D

Four coordinates

A four-dimensional mathematical space uses four independent coordinates. A tesseract is the four-dimensional analogue of a cube, though diagrams of it are projections into fewer dimensions.

H

Quaternions

A quaternion has four real components, often written a+bi+cj+dk. Unit quaternions provide an elegant way to encode rotations in three-dimensional space.

4 colors

Planar maps

The Four Color Theorem guarantees that four colors are enough for any planar map when regions sharing a boundary segment must receive different colors.

Be

Beryllium

Beryllium has atomic number 4, meaning every beryllium nucleus contains four protons.

00–11

Two bits

Because 4=2², exactly four two-bit binary patterns exist: 00, 01, 10, and 11. One quaternary digit can therefore carry the same combinatorial state count as two bits.

N E S W

Cardinal directions

North, east, south, and west form the conventional four cardinal directions on many maps. This is a human geographic convention layered onto continuous physical direction.

4/4

Musical meter

In common Western notation, 4/4 meter groups each notated measure around four quarter-note beats. It is extremely common, but it is a musical convention rather than a universal law of music.

Four letters, exponential sequence space

DNA uses four nucleotide bases—A, C, G, and T. If we ignore biological constraints and ask only a combinatorial question, a sequence of length n over a four-letter alphabet has 4ⁿ possible strings. Ten positions already allow 4¹⁰=1,048,576 possible strings. Biology does not sample those possibilities uniformly, but the counting principle explains why a four-symbol alphabet can encode immense diversity.

From square to tetrahedron

In two dimensions, the square is the regular shape most visibly associated with four: four equal sides and four right angles. In three dimensions, the tetrahedron brings another kind of fourfold structure. It has four vertices, six edges, and four triangular faces. Unlike a cube, it is a simplex: the minimal convex polytope with volume in three dimensions.

From tetrahedron to tesseract

A tesseract can be constructed conceptually by taking a cube and extending it in a new independent direction, just as a cube can be imagined by extending a square out of its plane. The tesseract has 16 vertices, 32 edges, 24 square faces, and 8 cubical cells. A drawing showing a “cube inside a cube” is only a projection; it is not literally a four-dimensional object on the screen.

Four components that rotate three-dimensional space

Hamilton's quaternions form a number system with basis 1,i,j,k. A quaternion contains four real coefficients. Although quaternion multiplication is not commutative, unit quaternions are especially useful for describing 3D rotations because they avoid some singularities that arise in other rotation parameterizations.

Four colors: a maximum, not a command

The Four Color Theorem says every planar map can be colored with at most four colors under the usual adjacency rule. Many particular maps need fewer. Also, touching only at a point does not normally make two regions adjacent for the theorem. “Four” is an upper bound guaranteed for all planar maps, not the required number for each map.

Four in chemistry

Atomic number counts protons. Beryllium is element 4, so every beryllium atom has a nucleus with four protons. Isotopes can differ in neutron count, and ions can differ in electron count; the proton count is what fixes the element's atomic number.

Four states and information

Any four-state symbol system carries log₂4=2 bits of information per equally likely symbol. That makes base 4 mathematically convenient when translating to binary: 0₄↔00₂, 1₄↔01₂, 2₄↔10₂, 3₄↔11₂. This exact two-bit correspondence is a consequence of 4 being a power of 2.

Museum note

Connections need causes

A square has four sides because of its definition. DNA has four standard bases because of molecular biology. Beryllium has atomic number 4 because it has four protons. The museum puts them in one room because the same number appears—not because one phenomenon causes the others.

Information plaque
4ⁿ = 2²ⁿ

Every quaternary digit corresponds to two binary bits. Four-state branching and binary branching are therefore exactly convertible at the level of state counts.
