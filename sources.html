Sources.

The museum separates stable mathematical facts from historical interpretation, distinguishes notation from number, and flags conventions or simplified models where they matter.

Fact-checking convention notes

“Natural number” is convention-dependent: some authors include 0, others begin at 1. The exhibition therefore prefers “first positive integer” when making a convention-independent statement. Historical claims about glyph evolution are phrased as broad lineage rather than a simplistic one-shape-to-one-shape chain.

Gallery 002 fact-checking notes

“Only even prime” follows directly from the modern definition of prime and the fact that every even integer greater than 2 has 2 as a proper divisor. The museum distinguishes the abstract number 2 from the modern glyph “2,” and it distinguishes securely known mathematical results from colorful but weakly documented origin legends.

Gallery 003 fact-checking notes

The museum separates the mathematical number 3 from its glyph and from cultural symbolism. The three-body SVG is an illustrative installation rather than a claimed numerical orbit. RGB is described as a common engineering color model, not as a statement that nature contains exactly three fundamental colors. Ordinary three-dimensional Euclidean coordinates are distinguished from higher-dimensional mathematical spaces and four-dimensional spacetime models.

Method

Museum standard

Mathematical claims should be definition-aware. Historical claims should distinguish evidence from attractive origin stories. Scientific analogies should say when a picture is illustrative rather than a simulation. Every new number exhibit should follow the same rule.
