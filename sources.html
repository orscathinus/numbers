Fact-checking convention notes

“Natural number” is convention-dependent: some authors include 0, others begin at 1. The exhibition therefore prefers “first positive integer” when making a convention-independent statement. Historical claims about glyph evolution are phrased as broad lineage rather than a simplistic one-shape-to-one-shape chain.

Gallery 002 · Number 2

Wolfram MathWorld — “2” and “Even Prime”

References for 2 as the second positive integer, first prime, even number, unique even prime, and the identity 2!=2. Number 2 · Even Prime

OEIS A000079 — Powers of 2

Reference for the sequence 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, … and standard combinatorial interpretations including the 2^n subsets of an n-element set. Open source

Wolfram MathWorld — “Pythagoras's Constant” and “Irrational Number”

References for √2≈1.41421356…, its role as the diagonal of the unit square, its irrationality, and its periodic continued fraction [1;2,2,2,…]. The legendary Hippasus story is treated as legend, not documentary fact. Pythagoras's Constant · Irrational Number

MacTutor History of Mathematics — “Indian numerals”

Historical reference for Brahmi numeral traditions from around the middle of the third century BCE, the use of repeated one-symbol forms for early 2 and 3, and later development through Gupta and Nagari forms. Open source

MacTutor History of Mathematics — “The Arabic numeral system”

Reference for transmission of Indian numerals through Arabic-speaking scholarly worlds, regional numeral variation, manuscript evidence, the Codex Vigilanus of 976, and the gradual European adoption of Hindu–Arabic numerals. Open source

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — “Pythagoreanism”

Used to keep the history of irrationality appropriately cautious. The article notes that no ancient source explicitly connects Hippasus with the discovery of irrationality; modern claims assigning him the discovery are disputed reconstructions. Open source

Gallery 002 fact-checking notes

“Only even prime” follows directly from the modern definition of prime and the fact that every even integer greater than 2 has 2 as a proper divisor. The museum distinguishes the abstract number 2 from the modern glyph “2,” and it distinguishes securely known mathematical results from colorful but weakly documented origin legends.

Method

Museum standard

Mathematical claims should be definition-aware. Historical claims should distinguish evidence from attractive origin stories. Every new number exhibit should follow the same rule.
