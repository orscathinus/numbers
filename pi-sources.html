Sources for π.

The π wing separates exact mathematical statements, historical evidence, computational observations, and unresolved questions such as normality.

NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions — Mathematical Constants

Primary reference for π = 3.1415926535… as the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter and for analytic definitions such as π = 4∫₀¹dt/(1+t²). Open source

NIST DLMF — Trigonometric functions and zeta values

Reference for standard trigonometric values at rational multiples of π and for ζ(2)=π²/6, ζ(4)=π⁴/90, and the general appearance of π in positive even zeta values. Trigonometry · Zeta values

Wolfram MathWorld — Pi

Reference for the circle ratio, decimal expansion, Archimedes' bounds, William Jones's notation, irrationality, Lindemann's transcendence result, and the fact that normality of π has not been proved. Open source

MacTutor — A history of Pi / chronology of π

Historical references for early approximations, Archimedes, Liu Hui, Zu Chongzhi, al-Kāshī, later European calculations, William Jones, and the growth of computation. History · Chronology

MacTutor — Zu Chongzhi

Reference for Zu Chongzhi's approximation 355/113 and its remarkable accuracy. Open source

MacTutor — earliest uses of π

Reference for William Jones's 1706 use of π for the circumference-to-diameter ratio and the earlier, evolving notation surrounding circle ratios. Open source

Fact-checking notes

22/7 and 355/113 are approximations, not exact values. Irrationality does not imply normality or digit randomness. The Monte Carlo and Buffon exhibits are statistical demonstrations, not efficient high-precision algorithms. The visual installations are explanatory artwork, not numerical simulations unless explicitly labeled as interactive computations.

Special-exhibit standard

Claims about π's digits are especially easy to exaggerate. This museum distinguishes what has been proved from what has merely been observed computationally.
