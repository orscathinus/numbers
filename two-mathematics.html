Classification wall

What kind of number is 2?

Two belongs to many overlapping sets, but a few classifications distinguish it from every other positive integer.

ℕ

Natural

Two is a positive counting number and the successor of 1.

ℤ

Integer

It lies exactly two unit steps to the right of 0 on the integer number line.

ℚ

Rational

2 = 2/1, so it is a ratio of integers with nonzero denominator.

2k

Even

2 = 2·1. More generally, divisibility by 2 is the definition of evenness.

p

Prime

Its only positive divisors are 1 and 2. It is the smallest prime number.

★

Unique even prime

Every even integer greater than 2 is divisible by 2 and therefore composite.

2!

Factorial fixed point

2! = 2·1 = 2. Among positive integers, only 1 and 2 equal their own factorials.

10₂

Binary “ten”

In base 2, decimal 2 is written 10: one two and zero ones.

√2

Irrational neighbor

The square root of 2 is irrational even though 2 itself is rational and integral.

A proof that 2 is the only even prime

Suppose an even integer n is greater than 2. Because n is even, n=2k for some integer k. Since n>2, we have k>1. So 2 is a divisor of n that is neither 1 nor n. Therefore n is composite. The only even positive integer that escapes this argument is 2 itself, whose positive divisors are exactly 1 and 2.

Parity is arithmetic modulo 2

The even–odd distinction can be expressed through remainders modulo 2. Every integer is congruent to either 0 or 1 mod 2. That turns parity into the simplest nontrivial modular arithmetic system: even integers occupy the residue class 0, odd integers the residue class 1. From this, rules such as even+even=even, odd+odd=even, and odd×odd=odd follow immediately.

The exponent of 2 inside an integer

For a nonzero integer n, the 2-adic valuation v₂(n) is the exponent of the highest power of 2 dividing n. For example, 40=2³·5, so v₂(40)=3. This measurement is fundamental in number theory because powers of 2 organize divisibility, congruences, and the arithmetic behavior of integers at the prime 2.

Powers of two

The sequence 2⁰,2¹,2²,… begins 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, …. It appears whenever each additional independent position has exactly two possibilities. An n-element set has 2ⁿ subsets because every element independently has two states: included or not included. Likewise, there are 2ⁿ binary strings of length n.

Arithmetic functions at 2

Several standard number-theoretic functions are easy to read off from the factorization of 2. The divisor-counting function gives τ(2)=2 because 2 has two positive divisors. The sum-of-divisors function gives σ(2)=1+2=3. Euler’s totient is φ(2)=1 because among the positive integers at most 2, only 1 is coprime to 2. The Möbius function gives μ(2)=−1 because 2 is a product of one distinct prime.

√2: a rational number gives birth to an irrational one

If a unit square has side length 1, its diagonal d satisfies d²=1²+1²=2, so d=√2. The classical proof of irrationality assumes √2=p/q in lowest terms. Squaring gives p²=2q², so p² is even and therefore p is even. Writing p=2r then forces q²=2r², making q even as well—a contradiction because p/q was assumed to be in lowest terms.

The decimal expansion begins 1.41421356237… and does not terminate or repeat periodically. Its simple continued fraction is strikingly repetitive: √2=[1;2,2,2,2,…]. Successive convergents 1, 3/2, 7/5, 17/12, 41/29, … provide increasingly accurate rational approximations.

Two in algebra

The cyclic group with two elements, often written C₂ or ℤ/2ℤ, is the smallest nontrivial group. Its nonidentity element is its own inverse. More generally, an involution is an operation or transformation that undoes itself when applied twice. Reflections are a familiar geometric example. This makes “order two” one of the first genuinely interesting symmetry structures in algebra.
