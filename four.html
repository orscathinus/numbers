4

Four. The first composite number, the first even perfect square, and the number that turns a line of factors into a square, a compass of directions, and four evenly spaced roots around a circle.

Four entrances

A number with corners.

Four is where multiplication first produces a nontrivial square and where prime arithmetic first gives way to composite structure. Its geometry and algebra repeatedly organize themselves into right angles, quadrants, and four-way cycles.

Arithmetic

The first composite

Four is the smallest positive integer greater than 1 that is not prime. Its factorization is 2², and its positive divisors are 1, 2, and 4. That makes it the first integer whose internal multiplicative structure is built from a repeated prime.

Geometry

The first even square

Four dots can form a 2-by-2 square. A square has four vertices, four sides, four right interior angles, and rotational symmetry through quarter-turns. In three dimensions, the regular tetrahedron is the smallest regular polyhedron and has four vertices and four triangular faces.

Complex plane

A compass of roots

The solutions to z⁴ = 1 are 1, i, −1, and −i. They lie at 90° intervals on the unit circle, forming the vertices of a square. Multiplication by i rotates the complex plane by one quarter-turn.

Representation

Four states at a time

Base 4 uses digits 0, 1, 2, and 3. Each additional quaternary digit multiplies the number of possible strings by 4. Because 4 = 2², every base-4 digit can also be represented exactly by two binary bits.

Composite structure begins

The primes 2 and 3 cannot be broken into smaller positive factors other than 1 and themselves. Four changes the pattern: 4 = 2 × 2. It is the smallest positive composite number. Its repeated prime factor also makes it the first positive integer greater than 1 that is a perfect square of another integer.

Its divisor functions are already more interesting than those of a prime: τ(4)=3 because 4 has three positive divisors; σ(4)=1+2+4=7; Euler's totient is φ(4)=2 because only 1 and 3 among 1,2,3,4 are relatively prime to 4.

Fourfold symmetry is not one thing

A square has four rotational positions before returning to its starting orientation: 0°, 90°, 180°, and 270°. Those rotations form the cyclic group C₄. If reflections are included too, the full symmetry group of a square is the dihedral group D₄, which has eight elements. The number 4 therefore appears both as the number of rotational states and as the order of C₄—not as the order of the square's full symmetry group.

Four is the first group order with a choice

Up through order 3, there is only one group up to isomorphism for each positive order. At order 4, two different group structures appear: the cyclic group C₄ and the Klein four-group C₂ × C₂. Both contain four elements, but their multiplication structures differ. Four is therefore the smallest order at which “a group with n elements” no longer determines the structure uniquely.

One number, many dimensions

The phrase “four-dimensional” means a space or model described by four independent coordinates; it does not mean that the number 4 itself is inherently physical. A tesseract is the four-dimensional analogue of a cube. It has 16 vertices, 32 edges, 24 square faces, and 8 cubical cells. Visual drawings of tesseracts are projections—lower-dimensional representations of a four-dimensional object.
